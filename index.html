Deal-killers & fine print — the 8 that matter here

  • The §121 clock is the biggest number on this page. If this was your primary residence 2 of the last 5 years, up to $250k/$500k of gain is tax-free — but only if you sell within ~3 years of moving out. Rent past that and the race table shows the cliff: on a ~$200k gain that's roughly $50k of tax you volunteer to pay for the privilege of landlording.
  • Depreciation is "allowed or allowable." The moment you rent, recapture liability accrues whether or not you claim the deduction — so claim it (it shields income at your marginal rate now; you'll owe ~25% fed recapture at sale regardless, unless you 1031 or die holding it).
  • AB 1482 exempts individually-owned single-family homes — only if you serve the statutory exemption notice in the lease. Miss the notice and you're capped at 5%+CPI with just-cause eviction rules. Get the lease language right on day one.
  • CA eviction reality: a non-paying tenant takes 3–6 months and $5k–15k in legal fees and lost rent, plus trash-out. One bad tenancy erases 1–2 years of cash flow at these margins — screen brutally or pay a manager who does.
  • Insurance before commitment: the CA landlord market is constrained; several carriers aren't writing. Get a bound DP-3 quote before choosing the rent path — FAIR Plan backup is expensive and thin.
  • Don't underwrite hope: 95207 is softening now (−3–5% YoY, DOM 53). Stockton's long-run averages hide a −65% drawdown in 2008. If the rent path only wins above ~4% appreciation (see breakeven chip), you're betting on the market, not the business.
  • Your Prop 13 assessment is an asset — while you hold. Your tax bill grows ≤2%/yr from your old basis; a buyer resets to ~1.15–1.25% of their price. Cheap taxes make holding more attractive for you specifically — use your actual bill above, not a market-rate guess. (Check for Mello-Roos/CFD lines on the bill.)
  • What's not modeled: income tax on rents (less depreciation shield) and on reinvestment yield — roughly a wash at typical rates but check with your CPA; 1031 optionality once it's an established rental; step-up at death if this is legacy money. This is analysis, not advice.
